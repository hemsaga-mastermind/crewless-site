Berth

Product · private

Reads the open job market and tells you which roles are worth your time — and, mostly, which are not, with the reason named.

Confidence

22/ 100

Our confidence it does what it claims, from internal testing. The machinery is proven end to end and measured. Nothing about demand is proven: it has had no users, and it was killed on our own product screen. A working tool and a business are different claims.

The idea

Job boards compete to show you more. But listings are free and infinite — what actually costs you is the evenings spent applying to roles that were never going to say yes.

So Berth is built to say no. It reads thousands of live postings, throws out the ones you cannot take or will not get, and explains each rejection. Most runs end with a handful of roles, and some end with none. That is the product working, not failing.

What it does

  • Reads a CV and extracts what it proves — skills, seniority, right-to-work — never what it guesses.
  • Asks you where you want to work. A CV says where you live, which is a different question. Searching India from Sweden is normal.
  • Fans out across six public job sources in parallel, merges and de-duplicates.
  • Judges each role on its own, citing the line in the posting that decided it, and names why they might turn you down.
  • If you give it your own LinkedIn export, it shows which of those companies you already know someone at. A referral beats a better-scoring cold application.

Every judgement must quote the posting it came from, and the quote is checked against that posting. When a model invents its evidence, the judgement is discarded rather than shown. An earlier build silently attributed one company’s text to another; that is exactly what the check now catches.

Where it looks

Every one is a public API published for programmatic reading. Nothing is scraped, no account is used, no password is ever asked for.

What it will not do

It will not take your LinkedIn password. Using someone’s credentials breaks LinkedIn’s terms and gets their account restricted. If you want your own profile and network read, you export your own data and hand us the file.

It will not keep your CV. It is read on our own machine and deleted when the run ends — including when the run fails. There is no CV database. We did not build one, deliberately.

It will not pad a list. If nothing fits this week it says so.

What it cannot see

LinkedIn, Naukri and Indeed cannot be read without breaking their terms, so we do not read them. Berth covers what it covers and says so. If you want those included, set up their own job alerts to your inbox — that is their product, working for you, at no cost.

Status

Private. Not open to the public, not sold, and not listed. It runs on our own hardware, behind a password, for a handful of people at a time while we find out whether the output is any good.

It remains killed on our product board. The kill condition is recorded and cheap to test, and it is about demand rather than capability: the tool working has never been the open question.
